## Days 1–4: Port Nyanzaru

### Day 1

I have crossed gulfs between worlds in which distance is largely a matter of opinion. Nevertheless, there is something uniquely disconcerting about teleportation. One moment we stood with Syndra; the next, Chult.

Heat. Noise. Smell. Life in unreasonable abundance.

After the comparative tranquillity of the Astral, Port Nyanzaru is an assault upon the senses. I mean that as observation rather than criticism. I rather like it.

Our first endeavours were gratifyingly mundane. We found lodgings, purchased supplies and ate extremely well. Some adventurers consider a fine meal before an expedition an extravagance. This is because adventurers are disproportionately represented amongst people who fail to consider that dinner may shortly consist of boiled roots beneath leaking canvas.

We visited the Temple of Savras and rendered assistance to one of its priests. The matter remains unfinished, so we shall return. Priests and rogues share at least one characteristic: neither forgets a debt.

There followed dinosaur racing.

We gambled.

We won.

I record this so that, should later entries document a catastrophic sequence of financial decisions, there remains evidence that our judgement was not _always_ defective.

Tomorrow we return to Syndra at Goldenthrone. We have also begun investigating guides prepared to lead us into the jungle.

Everyone talks about the jungle.

Everyone has advice.

Comparatively few of those offering it seem eager to accompany us.


### Day 2

More dinosaurs.

The fascination this city has for placing enormous reptiles near gambling opportunities is beginning to look less like novelty and more like municipal policy.

We returned to Goldenthrone and spoke with Syndra, Jobal and Ekene-afa. At the Temple of Savras we also spoke with Zitemwe concerning a parishioner named Draza, condemned to the Executioner's Run.

Naturally, we entered the Run.

We saved Draza. Celium died.

There are sentences one does not expect to place consecutively in a journal.

Resurrection was offered, which softened the finality if not the inconvenience.

Zitemwe has passed word through the Harpers that we seek Artus Cimber. Later Aazon arrived to conduct us around the available licensed guides.

It was, even by our standards, a fairly full day. 

### Day 3

There was business involving a warehouse. My records are disappointingly poor, which suggests either that nothing interesting occurred or that entirely too much did.

The evening is clearer.

We were warned that someone intended to take our map. Shortly thereafter, thugs entered our room armed with sleep venom and winged snakes.

Subtle.

The map went through a window and was caught by someone apparently resembling Aazon. We pursued him, only for his illusion to fail once he was caught.

I am professionally offended by elaborate deception executed badly. If one is going to manufacture another person's appearance, one should at least have a plan for what happens when somebody follows you.

The important point is simpler.

Someone wanted our map badly enough to send armed men for it.

### Day 4

We breakfasted with Zhanthi Ramas, who solved yesterday's mystery by informing us that she had acquired our map overnight.

For a price, naturally.

She would return it in exchange for first refusal on treasure we recover and offered free supplies if we assisted her. I suspect Zhentarim involvement. This is not necessarily condemnation. A thief may admire another thief whilst still counting the spoons afterwards.

We hired Azaka Stormfang as our guide and paid in advance for the necessary supplies.

Afterwards we returned to the Temple of Savras for healing potions and heard of a mysterious magical merchant named Zuberi.

We found him.

We bought things.

History may judge us.

# Into the Jungle

### Days 5–8

Four days upon the river.

My notes for this portion are sparse. There was, I believe, an encounter involving undead aboard or around a boat, though I refuse to improve an uncertain memory merely for literary satisfaction.

By the eighth day we had acquired a dinosaur.

I shall allow that sentence to stand without defence.

We reached a trading post and encountered Captain Vance, who had apparently taken to ambushing travellers upon the river. We disagreed with this practice sufficiently vigorously for the disagreement to become consequential.

We also learned that Camp Righteous was occupied again, though apparently not by undead.

In Chult, this qualifies as encouraging news. 

### Day 9

We reached Camp Righteous.

Its most striking feature is an enormous statue depicting a man carrying a crocodile upon his back. The camp itself was empty.

We found a corpse in the privy.

I have travelled the planes for a considerable time and have concluded that there is no dignity in death, merely varying degrees of inconvenience for whoever discovers the body.

The deceased possessed several gems, a little coin, a warhammer and scale armour.

A small temple nearby proved considerably more interesting, by which I mean it contained traps and underground chambers designed to kill the curious.

We emerged with an extraordinary jug capable of producing water, beer, honey and several substantially less drinkable substances.

Then goblins attacked.

Azaka Stormfang was grievously wounded.

Chult appears determined to alternate archaeology and attempted murder with admirable efficiency.

### Days 10–12

We abandoned most river travel and continued on foot with a single boat.

On the tenth day we discovered an abandoned camp. A storm arrived overnight, encouraging an unusually prompt departure.

The following day the trail had washed away. We improvised a system involving boat, pulley and supplies. A raptor watched the proceedings while Azaka Stormfang attempted to distract it.

I sometimes wonder what the great elven explorers of earlier ages would make of us.

Probably detailed notes.

That night we encountered Artus Cimber, the man we had been seeking. He warned us about our guide. He is also, rather unmistakably, the man associated with the supernatural cold.

On the twelfth day we encountered a grung carrying a fragment of map. Evidence suggested Zhentarim activity near Ataaz Kahakla.

The jungle is beginning to feel considerably less empty than the maps imply. 
### Day 13

We arrived at Camp Vengeance.

Captain Niles Breakbone wished to see us. I also met Captain Salhana, who appears reassuringly sane, and Ord Firebeard, the camp's chaplain and healer.

Nangalore was recommended as our next destination. Captain Salhana agreed.

Teron Yudan, a heavily armoured human soldier, became noteworthy enough that I recorded an embarrassingly complete assessment of his physical capabilities.

Old habits.

If you travel with dangerous people, it is prudent to know precisely _how_ dangerous they are.

# Nangalore

### Days 14–16

On the road we encountered a lobster-like humanoid who gestured towards Nangalore. That night we observed a zombie travelling north-west and later found a dead man with a hole in his chest from which orchids were growing.

Beauty and horror coexist here without apparently finding the arrangement peculiar.

We learned more of Nangalore: the Hanging Gardens of Dreams, associated with an exiled Omu-an queen.

A medusa.

We arrived on the sixteenth day.

Crocodiles attacked us. A yellow musk zombie became involved, though it eventually withdrew. Upon a wall we found the serene visage of a woman and damaged writing referring, perhaps, to love and to ten years of splendour.

More yellow musk zombies followed. Then a yellow musk creeper. Then a giant spider.

Then four giant snakes.

I increasingly suspect Chult considers the phrase _unoccupied ruin_ a contradiction in terms. 

### Days 17–18

We discovered a dead wizard and a folding boat.

Elsewhere we found a beetle capable of curing mad monkey disease, plants worth harvesting, and plants which objected rather strongly to being harvested. Several of us were psychically compelled towards seeking zombies.

There were giant birds.

There was a central dome and cistern.

There were tri-frond plants which nearly killed us while we attempted to gather their fruit.

A strange chwinga gave Menes a coin capable of greater restoration. We rested in the bird dome and recovered several gems and a scroll protecting against undead.

On the eighteenth day we passed through water into the final dome.

There we met Queen Zalkoré, and perhaps Thiru-Taya.

There was also an enormous black orchid.

Nangalore deserves its title. It is a garden of lost dreams.

I merely question whose dreams they were.

# Towards Omu

### Days 19–23

We sailed the River Olung.

The Red Wizards appeared with six skeletons and offered three choices: bypass them, join them, or die.

Diplomacy in Chult is admirably concise.

We nearly killed Zimara Vaal. Azaka Stormfang saved Ponty by transforming into a weretiger.

This explained several things.

We fought skeletons, zombies and an ogre zombie before retreating during a third wave of attackers.

Later we reached a cliff monastery by way of a walkway seemingly designed by someone hostile to the concept of walking. The aarakocra leader was Ashara. We learned of other “no-feathers” upon the plateau and passed a statue of Ubtao.

At the monastery we met the elderly monk Chiry, Princess Mwaxanaré and her brother.

There were tablets concerning Omu.

From them I pieced together a history: Omu rose from a place where the land was thin. Seven trickster beings became gods. Fed upon blood and praise, they grew louder while Ubtao grew quieter. Omu dimmed, and the jungle remembered.

Five animal representations had been defaced.

There were warnings about walking lightly where gods once walked.

There were also gargoyles.

The monastery itself was attacked.

Omu lies to the south-west.

### Days 24–28

Ashara told us that gargoyle attacks were increasing, presumably originating from Omu. A ritual could grant us flight.

We returned to Nangalore, encountered hostile creatures and plants, and found Neil transformed into stone.

By the bridge we discovered a mural bearing cryptic instructions concerning rhythm, breath and dawn.

Returning to Kir Sabal, we undertook the cleansing ritual.

_Flight is not a gift._

_The dead do not hold you down, only the living._

There is sufficient philosophy in those words to occupy several centuries. Unfortunately we had monsters to kill.

We flew to Firefinger, found Slate wounded and rescued him, then fought our way into the caves after being spotted. Stirges and pterafolk followed.

The next day we reached the summit and killed the remaining pterafolk. We rescued Nephyr, who promised us favour in future dealings with the aarakocra.

Azaka Stormfang recovered her holy mask and left us to return to her people.

I had grown accustomed to her presence.

One should be cautious about becoming accustomed to anyone while adventuring. The world interprets attachment as a challenge. [[onedrive.live.com\|onedrive.live.com]](https://onedrive.live.com?cid=03dff87c3dbf558c&id=3DFF87C3DBF558C!sde5499dfff834379b297941ab0e26b89)

### Days 29–32

We flew back towards the trading post and found another Red Wizard with skeleton attendants purchasing supplies.

The following day we discovered the wreck of the Star Goddess.

Ghouls attacked amongst the wreckage. Four-armed zombie gorillas joined them. Teron Yudan fell beneath the assault, though we eventually destroyed the creatures and rescued the survivors.

Then a dragon took Teron Yudan.

There are days when chronology itself sounds drunk.

We followed to Wyrmheart Mine, infiltrated past kobolds and reached the depths, where we negotiated with the dragon, Tinder. One thousand gold bought Teron Yudan's return.

Slate subsequently arrived in a cart and upset the dragon.

Tinder demanded a trinket from the dwarven forges of Hrakhamar.

We then sought the Heart of Ubtao: an immense heart-shaped stone floating in the air, bearing a single tree.

There we gained valuable knowledge. Omu lies directly south. Yuan-ti patrol between it and the Heart. They resist ordinary weapons. Undead and ancient traps infest the city.

Magical weapons would be advisable.

I carry mine in my thoughts.

For once, this peculiarity seems less eccentric than practical. [[onedrive.live.com\|onedrive.live.com]](https://onedrive.live.com?cid=03dff87c3dbf558c&id=3DFF87C3DBF558C!sde5499dfff834379b297941ab0e26b89)

### Days 33–35

We flew towards Hrakhamar.

Malkor Stonegrist, an albino dwarf, told us that fire newts had taken the forge. We found extraordinary quantities of adamantine and a dwarven trade-route map showing Omu.

More interesting was a dark, cauldron-like crucible.

_The heart beats where there is no life._

_The fire is eternal._

Its purpose appeared necromantic. It seemed to respond to the proximity of undead.

During watch, something scryed upon us.

The next day we returned and cleared more of Hrakhamar before continuing towards Omu. The crucible pulsed as we approached.

On the thirty-fifth day we fought yuan-ti amongst ruins and found a map showing a north-eastern entrance to Omu through a waterfall. We recovered healing potions, part of a Red Wizard's journal and a token marked with snake and sword.

The journal warned of serpent factions and advised avoidance of the central shrines.

Advice regarding ancient cursed cities is traditionally most useful immediately before circumstances make following it impossible.

# Omu

### Day 36

Omu began with a vision.

Blackness erupted across the city, though what I saw belonged to its past.

The guardhouse walls bore graffiti:

_Fear the fangs of Ras Nsi._

_The puzzle cubes are the key._

_All hail the King of Feathers._

_The snakes are not what they seem._

We found an entire Red Wizard camp dead, and beneath rubble discovered Orvex Ocrammas.

He knew the history of Omu and its nine trickster gods, whose rivalries were preserved in the shrines. The Red Wizards had been searching for the puzzle cubes needed to enter the Tomb of the Nine Gods.

We investigated Kubazan's shrine.

Its lesson was straightforward: tread without fear and give back as much as one takes.

Then we killed a froghemoth.

Shagambi's shrine taught that evil should be fought with honour.

We eventually fled its golems.

That night the yuan-ti ambushed us.

I am beginning to think every moral lesson in Omu concludes with something attempting to eat the student. [[onedrive.live.com\|onedrive.live.com]](https://onedrive.live.com?cid=03dff87c3dbf558c&id=3DFF87C3DBF558C!seaa75329376c495692d792f37e8c1459)

### Days 37–40

We returned to Shagambi's challenge and discovered its mechanism: defeat the golems, take their spears and use those spears against the statues controlling them.

We earned Shagambi's cube.

We also encountered Bag of Nails, a tabaxi hunter searching for an ancient jewel called the Navel of the Moon. He believed it could help him find his son.

At midnight we discovered that Bag of Nails had poisoned our stew.

I have written before about the danger of attachment.

Dinner, apparently, deserves similar caution.

We subsequently conquered the shrines of Unkh and Obo'laka. One urged us to contrast all choices before acting. The other instructed us to tread warily and remain in the light.

There were ghasts, zorbos, elaborate torch puzzles and a petrified Thayan.

At Papazotl's shrine we found the cube missing. We suspected the Red Wizards.

The following day brought Kazim Nul, another wizard, who did not wish to talk.

We nearly died.

Zagmira, leader of the Red Wizards, was encountered at Wongo's shrine, whose puzzle concerned friendship, hunger and a collection of animal figures.

I confess there are moments when stabbing something with a blade made from pure thought feels much simpler than archaeology. [[onedrive.live.com\|onedrive.live.com]](https://onedrive.live.com?cid=03dff87c3dbf558c&id=3DFF87C3DBF558C!seaa75329376c495692d792f37e8c1459)

### Day 41

I dreamt of a dark figure approaching me.

It pointed.

I dislike dreams that behave like memories.

I dislike visions that behave like visitors.

Most of all, I dislike discovering that something knows where to find me.

Later there was a teleporting tyrannosaur.

I shall not elaborate. The phrase is sufficient.

We recovered an Omu-an death mask and a helm capable of telepathy. We also found a decree of Queen Napaka, proclaiming that Omu feared nothing and that its new gods would protect it as Ubtao once had.

Ruins possess a peculiar talent for making confidence look foolish.

At I'jin's shrine we were instructed to take the path least expected and solved another sequence involving animal symbols.

Later we saw Zagmira conducting a ritual in the market.

Then the dark figure came to me again.

This time it spoke.

**The Soulmonger has come.**

My mind has always been the one place I considered indisputably mine.

### Day 42

We awoke expecting an ambush.

Instead we found a dead yuan-ti bearing the symbol of the Night Serpent and a message from Zagmira. She proposed a meeting at sunset upon the western roof of the House of the Crocodile.

Before that meeting we entered Moa's shrine.

_Moa teaches us that secrets hide the truth._

A serpent constricted a monkey in the mosaic. Warriors aimed bows towards the pedestal. The inscription was characteristically unhelpful.

We secured the cube.

At sunset we met Zagmira.

Then the snakes attacked.

During the chaos Lady Shadowmantle, a death witch, appeared and took Zagmira away. One of her cubes fell into yuan-ti hands.

### Day 43

We entered beneath the palace through a secret door.

There were supplies, temple incense, zombies and something capable of creating more zombies.

We wisely withdrew from a pit filled with snakes.

Further within stood a cathedral with fountains of blood.

I mention these details because there are occasions when architecture provides an entirely adequate moral assessment of its occupants.

We overheard discussions of Fenthaza, Dendar the Night Serpent, Ras Nsi and the puzzle cubes. The yuan-ti understood that all nine cubes would open the tomb. They feared what lay there, yet also feared outsiders reaching it.

We learned that Dendar's release would plunge the world into darkness.

Then matters deteriorated.

We encountered a hydra.

Neil was compelled to flee. Pete was captured.

Attempting to recover our companions led eventually to guards overwhelming almost the entire party. Teron Yudan alone remained standing.

Pete woke in a pit among an improbable collection of prisoners: a praying boy, a yuan-ti, a tabaxi bard, a fire newt warrior, a sailor from the Star Goddess, a Harper, a goblin, a tribal warrior and a Zhentarim woman.

There was also, apparently, a shop selling objects with names including the Compass of Bad Decisions, Boots of the Indignant Tapir, Pocket Monsoon, Fireflies of Questionable Allegiance and Self-Important Rope.

I resent the fact that the Compass of Bad Decisions would be useful to us.

### Day 44

Reconnaissance revealed guards, malisons and the palace layout. We gathered rumours concerning Fenthaza and learnt more of Ras Nsi's defences.

Corvyn and Menes reached a teleportation circle.

Menes arrived amongst the sacrifice pits.

Corvyn arrived by the Iron Throne.

There are methods of reconnaissance which produce information.

There are methods which produce anecdotes.

Against expectation, both survived.

Eventually Ras Nsi received us.

The situation was stranger than anticipated.

He is cursed. He made a pact with the lich Acererak to protect the tomb. Acererak had promised that the arrangement would help bar Dendar from entering the world.

The tomb had been built by slaves.

And somewhere within it lay the source of the Death Curse we had come to destroy.

Ras Nsi agreed to have us escorted there.

I distrust him.

I also distrust Acererak.

# Day 45: The Tomb

We stand outside the Tomb of the Nine Gods.

There is an obelisk.

Upon it is Acererak's warning, proclaiming himself eternal and foretelling the night when some forsaken one takes death's mantle, the dead rise, and he reaps the living.

There are further instructions, riddles perhaps.

Enemies oppose one another.

One stands between them.

Something hides in darkness.

A mask prevents one from being seen.

We are warned to speak no truth to a doomed child.

And the keys turn on the inside.

I have spent much of this expedition recording warnings left by dead civilisations.

Do not enter.

Walk carefully.

Stay in the light.

Beware the serpent.

Fear the fangs.

Do not trust.

Do not take.

Turn back.

It is tempting to laugh at them. Our profession depends, to an embarrassing degree, upon discovering a warning carved into stone and interpreting it as an invitation.

But these words do not feel ancient.

They feel expectant.

Behind us lies Port Nyanzaru, the river, Camp Righteous, Nangalore, Kir Sabal, Firefinger, Hrakhamar and ruined Omu. Ahead is a tomb constructed by slaves for dead gods, guarded at the command of a lich, containing something capable of corrupting death itself. My companions make their preparations.

Steel is inspected. Spells are considered. Packs rearranged. People perform the little rituals by which thinking creatures persuade themselves they exercise some authority over what happens next.

I have no blade to sharpen.

I close my hand and allow thought to acquire an edge.

The familiar pressure answers.

Silent.

Weightless.

Mine.

For forty-five days Chult has tried to teach us caution. We have listened with varying degrees of competence.

Perhaps that is why we are still alive.

Or perhaps the distinction between courage and failing to die has always been largely retrospective.

I look once more at Acererak's warning.

Then at the entrance.

There is an old habit amongst scholars of placing a neat conclusion at the end of an account. A summation. An observation made wiser by everything that preceded it.

I have none.

We are going inside.

That will have to suffice.
